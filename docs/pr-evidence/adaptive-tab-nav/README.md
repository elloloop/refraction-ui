# Adaptive Tab Nav review captures

Docs-site pages in light and dark, rendered by the Next.js dev server with Chromium (reduced motion). `before-*` is the Bottom Nav page at the parent revision: the library's existing tab bar, which is fixed to the bottom and hidden from 768px up, so a top-bar layout needs a second, separate navigation for wide screens. `after-*` is the new Adaptive Tab Nav page at 1440px and 375px; at 375px the demo's nav has left its top bar and is docked to the bottom of the viewport. `after-docked-*` is the 375px viewport scrolled to the demo, showing the docked bar over the page. Bottom Nav and Navbar are unchanged.

`browser-proof.json` records, for every capture, page overflow and browser errors, and on the Adaptive Tab Nav page what the browser measured on the demo. At 1440px: the nav is in the top bar and not positioned; the first tab is reached with Tab, draws its focus ring, and Tab moves on in reading order; Enter and Space choose a tab, move `aria-current="page"` to it and keep focus on it; the tabs are 44px tall; the active label's contrast against its pill is 8.38:1 in light and 7.17:1 in dark; choosing "Profile", which belongs to no section, lights no tab. At 375px: the same single nav element is fixed with its bottom edge on the viewport's, at x 0 and the full 375px width, with 12px of bottom padding (more where the device reports a home-indicator inset); its three tabs are 44px tall and equally wide, with the icon above the label; Enter chooses a tab; widening the viewport to 1280px returns the nav to the top bar and narrowing it docks it again, without a reload and keeping the selection.

The browser error in dark mode is a hydration mismatch in the docs site's own colour-mode toggle (one per page load), present on the parent revision too. Nothing else is logged.
